# Editorial voice contract

**Master tagline:** **Zero players. Zero developers. Progress continues regardless.**

This is the project-owned voice for the unofficial, AI-assisted continuation of
*Progress Quest*. The machine treats autonomous progress as institutional
policy. The joke may decorate a fact; it must never replace or distort one.

## The four registers

### Literal mechanical record

Use terse, exact language for game state, item effects, saves, failures,
diagnostics, privacy, accessibility, releases, credits, and provenance. Say what
happened, what changed, and what the user can do. Mechanical facts stay literal.

### Pompous institutional interpretation

Use restrained administrative overconfidence around a literal fact: offices,
committees, procurement, promotions, classifications, and procedures staffed by
nobody. The institution—not the user—is the target. This is the normal comic
register for interface framing, empty states, updates, and release commentary.

### Terse MMO/MUD world texture

Use short, clearly fictional local notices to imply zones, guilds, raids, town
services, and other persistent-world conventions. Keep canonical Progress Quest
state authoritative. Never imply that simulated characters are real people or
that a networked multiplayer service exists.

### Rare cosmic escalation

Reserve impossible scale and existential bureaucracy for infrequent milestones.
It should escalate a truthful event, not become the default voice. If every empty
slot threatens the universe, the universe becomes ordinary inventory.

## Approved supporting family

The master tagline is stable and numeral-independent. Supporting lines may be
used sparingly and must not compete with it:

- Built by nobody. Played by nobody. Operating normally.
- The staffing target was zero. The progress target was not.
- Now serving zero concurrent players across every supported viewport.
- No players were interrupted during this deployment.

The current title is deliberately outside this contract; issue #140 owns naming
and repository/Pages migration.

## Truth and credit boundary

The fictional slogan never erases real work. Visible credits must distinguish:

- Eric Fredricksen as original creator and principal author of the retained web
  reference, while preserving its contributor and third-party attribution;
- William Zujkowski's human project direction and review; and
- AI-assisted research, implementation, and testing.

Errors lead with the failure and recovery action. Update and release notices keep
the exact deployed commit and verification result. Accessibility-critical text,
including control names and status meaning, stays literal. Jokes must remain
brief, optional where practical, and subordinate to the information.

## Prohibited-reference checklist

Reject copy, prompts, code, or assets containing any of the following:

- named-creator style prompts;
- protected characters, settings, or catchphrases;
- copied or conspicuously similar joke structures;
- unlicensed donor text, code, or assets; or
- false authorship or provenance claims.

Inspiration supplies techniques such as anti-climax, procedural escalation, and
institutional absurdity—not recognizable expression. Research citations do not
grant reuse rights.

## Generated-copy review

Authored catalogs remain deterministic, bounded, and mechanically truthful.
Whenever a generated-copy catalog changes, its tests must reject source names or
catchphrases, suspiciously long overlaps, unbounded output, and mechanical claims
the engine does not support. Those checks require human review; they are not
license clearance. Runtime generation, chat, and optional local models must also
retain a deterministic authored fallback and zero authority over game state.

## Surface checklist

Before merging changed copy:

1. Identify its register and keep the literal fact visible.
2. Verify authorship, provenance, privacy, and mechanical claims.
3. Check its accessible name and status meaning by keyboard and screen-reader
   semantics; do not make accessibility needs the joke.
4. Check 320 px mobile and the supported desktop dashboard for overflow or lost
   controls.
5. Update stable assertions when intentional visible wording changes.
6. Use the release-note template, recording facts before optional commentary.
